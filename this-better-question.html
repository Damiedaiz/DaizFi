A better question

A small field guide for slowing down, separating what you know from what you assume, and choosing your next move with clarity.

Before you act, ask better.

Good decisions rarely begin with having every answer. They begin with asking a question that exposes what matters.

The CLEAR check
  1. Context: What is actually happening? Describe the situation without blame or drama.
  2. Limits: What do I know, and what am I assuming?
  3. Evidence: What can I verify? What would change my mind?
  4. Alternatives: What are two other explanations or possible actions?
  5. Response: What is the smallest useful next step I can test?

Use this one-minute prompt.

Situation: What happened, in one sentence?

Known: What facts can I point to?

Assumed: What am I filling in?

Next: What can I safely test today?

Write your answers somewhere you can revisit. A decision is stronger when you can explain why you made it.

Keep the habit.

Use CLEAR before sending a difficult message, spending money, accepting an offer, choosing a tool, or repeating something you heard. It is not a guarantee. It is a way to make your thinking visible.
